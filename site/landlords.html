Getting a rental ready for 2030

Two things are coming for every private landlord in England: a higher energy standard by 2030, and a national register of rental homes, which opens in the South East in March 2027.

EPC C for every tenancy by 1 October 2030

The government has confirmed that privately rented homes must reach EPC C for all tenancies from 1 October 2030. Until then the current minimum, EPC E, still applies. The rules still need legislation, which the government intends to bring in during 2027.

  • Spending cap: £10,000 a property, or 10% of its value if that’s lower. Money spent from 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2029 counts, except on fossil fuel heating.
  • If you reach the cap and the home still isn’t at C, you can register a cost cap exemption, valid for 10 years.
  • Other exemptions cover work that would harm the property, or where you’d rather not fit solid wall insulation. These are time-limited.
  • Fines go up to £30,000 per breach.
  • An EPC C you already have, if achieved before 1 October 2029, stays valid until it expires.

The rental register: South East from 15 March 2027

Landlords must register themselves and each occupied rental on the government’s new service, which opens region by region. For the South East, which includes Sussex and Kent, registration runs from 15 March to 14 June 2027. The government’s council lookup confirms which region you’re in.

  • Fee: £65 a year per property, reduced during the rollout. Registering as a landlord is free.
  • Penalties: up to £7,000 for not registering, and up to £40,000 for false information or repeated breaches.

What usually moves a home up the bands

Insulation, better glazing, a heat pump and solar panels are the usual routes, and the right order depends on the property. An EPC assessor or a qualified installer can tell you which measures yours needs. From March 2027 our directory will show which installers take on rental work.

General information only, not advice on a particular property. Rules may change before 2030, which is why we date every check.

Common questions

When do rentals need EPC C?

For all tenancies from 1 October 2030, under the government’s confirmed plans. The legislation is due in 2027.

How much do landlords have to spend?

Up to £10,000 a property, or 10% of its value if lower. If it still isn’t at C, a cost cap exemption can be registered.

When do Sussex and Kent landlords register?

The South East registration period runs from 15 March to 14 June 2027. The fee is £65 a year per property.
