Solar panels, in plain English

Panels on your roof make electricity whenever it’s light. You use what you can and can be paid for what you send back, and fitting is VAT-free until 31 March 2027.

What the size really means

Systems are described by their peak output in kilowatts, written kWp: the most they produce in full sun. What actually affects your bills is how much they make over a year, which depends on the way your roof faces, its pitch and any shade. Ask for the yearly estimate in kilowatt hours (kWh), and how it was worked out.

VAT-free until March 2027

Solar panels fitted to a home in Great Britain are zero-rated for VAT until 31 March 2027. The reduced rate applies from 1 April 2027.

Getting paid for what you export

Under the Smart Export Guarantee, energy suppliers pay you for electricity you send back to the grid, and most only do so if the system was fitted by an MCS-certified installer. Ask for the installer’s MCS certificate number, and for your system’s certificate when the job’s done.

Five questions for every quote

  1. Generation: how many kWh a year do you expect, and how did you calculate it?
  2. Equipment: which panels and inverter, and what are the warranties on each?
  3. The roof: is scaffolding included, and how will you check the roof first?
  4. The network: will you notify the local electricity network operator for me?
  5. Later: can a battery be added without changing the inverter?

Wattwell gives general information only, not advice on a particular home.

Common questions

Is there VAT on solar panels right now?

No. Fitting them to a home in Great Britain is zero-rated until 31 March 2027, and the reduced rate applies from 1 April 2027.

Do I need an MCS installer for solar panels?

Most suppliers need an MCS-certified installation before they’ll pay you for exported electricity, so it’s worth insisting on.

What does kWp mean?

Kilowatt peak: the most the system produces in full sun. Ask for the yearly figure in kWh as well.
