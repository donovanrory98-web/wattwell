Home batteries, and when they pay

A battery stores electricity for later: your own solar from the afternoon, or cheaper power overnight on the right tariff. Fitting is VAT-free until 31 March 2027.

When a battery earns its place

With solar panels, a battery keeps the power you’d otherwise export cheaply, so you can use it in the evening. Without solar, it can charge on a cheaper overnight tariff and run the house through the expensive hours. Whether it pays off depends on when you use electricity, so ask the installer to show the numbers for your home.

VAT-free until March 2027

Home batteries have been zero-rated for VAT since 1 February 2024, including batteries fitted on their own. The zero rate runs to 31 March 2027, and the reduced rate applies from 1 April 2027.

Five questions for every quote

  1. Usable capacity: how many kWh can I actually use, not just the headline size?
  2. Power: how many kW can it deliver at once? Will it run the oven and the kettle together?
  3. Warranty: how many years or cycles, and how much capacity is guaranteed at the end?
  4. Tariff: which tariff are the savings based on, and what if it changes?
  5. Power cuts: will it keep the house running in a cut, or is that extra?

Wattwell gives general information only, not advice on a particular home.

Common questions

Is there VAT on a battery fitted on its own?

No, until 31 March 2027: batteries are zero-rated even without solar panels.

Is a battery worth it without solar panels?

It can be, on a tariff with cheaper overnight electricity. Ask the installer to show the savings for your own usage.

What size battery do I need?

That depends on how much electricity you use in the evening. Ask for the usable capacity in kWh, not just the headline size.
